## Works
- Traffic, ClearanceLink side (not drawn in X-Plane yet): `serve` answers EnumerateSimObjectsAndLiveries from `mapping/liveries.json` (the 5,497 models MSFS listed), creates BATC's AI objects (AssignedObjectId), keeps their state from SetDataOnSimObject and events, answers BATC's ground-height polls (nearest served airport's elevation, else the ground under the user), and lists the traffic on the status page. Replay-tested against the recorded MSFS session (`TrafficTests`). Live on Ben's PC 2026-10-01 at YBBN: BATC created 45 aircraft and 3 tugs and its AI traffic talked on Ground. `--no-traffic` turns it off.
- Everyday use (0.3.0): `scripts/publish.ps1` builds a self-contained Windows zip (`ClearanceLink.exe`, the `FlightSimulator2024.exe` stub, `mapping/`, `README.txt`, two .cmd switches). Double-clicking `ClearanceLink.exe` offers to point BATC at it and then serves; `clearancelink batc on|off|status` adds or removes the two `SimConnect.cfg` redirects (BATC folder from its uninstall entry or `C:\BeyondATC`; an existing cfg is backed up and restored). Outside a source checkout, recordings go to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\ClearanceLink\recordings`. Package smoke-tested on Ben's PC 2026-10-01.
- Window (ADR-0006, not yet run on Ben's PC): double-clicking ClearanceLink.exe opens an FMS-style Avalonia window (STATUS, SETUP, SUPPORT, ABOUT; line select keys, keys 1-6 / Shift+1-6 / F1-F4, clicks on rows) that runs `serve` until closed. SETUP picks folders, connection mode, removes 0.3.0 cfg files, starts BeyondATC, opens the web page; SUPPORT toggles the session log, makes the support zip, shows the live log. The .cmd files are gone; `clearancelink start` keeps the console flow. Pages are tested from `CduPages.Build`; headless tests render every page (`CLEARANCELINK_SCREENSHOTS=<dir>` saves PNGs). Since the traffic work merged, STATUS shows how many aircraft the traffic add-on (the X-Plane plugin) is drawing and SETUP offers INSTALL>/UPDATE> for it (X-Plane must be closed), replacing the console start's install question.
- App icon (2026-10-04): `src/ClearanceLink.Host/Assets/ClearanceLink.svg` (a green datalink message bubble with a tick on a dark rounded square) is built into `ClearanceLink.ico` (16 to 256 px, each drawn from the SVG) by `scripts/make-icon.py`; it is the exe's icon (ApplicationIcon) and the window and live-log title bar icon.
- Getting started (not yet run on Ben's PC): until `gettingStartedDone` is saved in settings.json, the window opens on six WELCOME pages written as datalink-style messages (plain text above, line select keys only where there is something to press): no-warranty and not-affiliated notice, BeyondATC and X-Plane folders, X-Plane plugin install, aircraft models (Bluebell package count; FSLTL found through MSFS or chosen, CONVERT runs `fsltl import` in the background with X-Plane closed, converted livery count), X-Plane settings to turn down, every-flight order. SKIP or FINISH saves it; SUPPORT > SHOW AGAIN reopens them. User help is `docs/help.md` (linked from the window); README.md and packaging/README.txt are short and point there. After Ben's first test of 0.5.0 (2026-10-04): page 6 starts every flight with planning it in SimBrief (BATC loads the latest plan; the departure must be where X-Plane is parked) and no longer says to close MSFS (STATUS says so when it matters), page 5 names X-Plane's own settings (Sound > Radios, Graphics > Draw parked aircraft), ABOUT is a message like the WELCOME pages, and STATUS's LAST AIRPORT / FROM is now ATC AIRPORT / SCENERY.
- Automatic connection (ADR-0007): with `connection automatic` (the default), `serve` without `--listen` answers on MSFS's default pipe while the real MSFS is closed, plus `tcp:5112` for cfg-file set-ups. The status page says to close MSFS while it runs, and lists other MSFS add-ons as not used. `connection always-on` writes the cfg files and listens on TCP only. Pipe tested with BATC on Ben's PC on 2026-10-02 (both connections, gate, COM1); the MSFS step-aside is untested.

## Not yet verified
- `serve` in flight (only ground work to taxi is verified). Wind (`verify: true`) not compared with the cockpit; design speeds and wingspan are fixed values.
- MSFS starting while ClearanceLink is running (step-aside), and whether other add-ons connected over the pipe (SayIntentions, NG Academy readers) misbehave when they get no answers.
- X-Plane traffic plugin (ADR-0004, `plugin/`, protocol `docs/protocol/plugin.md`): built on XPMP2, draws aircraft sent by ClearanceLink with CSL models, puts ground traffic on X-Plane's terrain and reports terrain height back. Compiles and its protocol tests pass on Linux; Windows build runs in GitHub Actions. ClearanceLink side: `PluginLink` (UDP), `clearancelink plugin install|remove|status`, double-click start offers to install it, status page shows it. `TrafficBridge` sends BATC's aircraft (not tugs) to the plugin 10 times a second, with type and airline from `mapping/traffic-models.yaml`, and answers BATC's ground-height polls with the plugin's terrain under that aircraft (nearest airport until the plugin has measured). Live in X-Plane since 2026-10-01 with Bluebell CSL models. Plugin 0.4.0 parks each CSL model on its own wheels, posed with the gear down from its .obj files (checked against Ben's 2,027 Bluebell models); per-type pitch and height in mapping/traffic-models.yaml are only a fallback for models it cannot fit. Plugin 0.3.1 blends between updates (smooth turns and climbs) and probes terrain less often; ClearanceLink sends unchanged parked aircraft every 2 s instead of 10 times a second; 0.3.2 stops those repeats sliding parked aircraft back and forth. 0.4.1 also levels the bogies of models that tilt them on World Traffic's gear dataref (Bluebell's virtual-airline repaints), and removes aircraft whose `del` UDP dropped; ClearanceLink repeats `del` and sends `clear` when it starts. A packet that fails to decode or handle is logged and no longer drops BATC's connection. Aircraft on the ground are drawn wings level (BATC writes a bank on the take-off roll). 0.4.2 patches XPMP2 (plugin/cmake/xpmp2-split-packages.patch) so CSL packages split over several folders load all their models: XPMP2 3.7.0 looked for each .obj in the first folder only, so about 750 Bluebell models (B744_QFA, B772_UAL...) were missing and BATC's aircraft fell back to virtual-airline repaints (YBBN 2026-10-03, 2,283 "could not be found" lines in Log.txt). 0.4.2 also finds each bogie's level from where its wheels end up, which levels the 747's wing-gear bogies (modelled already tilted, turning 30° to 73°). 0.4.3 draws BATC placing an aircraft (a move far faster than its speed) at once, instead of sliding it and overshooting back and forth. Plugin 0.4.4 fits wheels on the nearest LOD only; 0.4.5 turns propellers, engines and tyres from their rpm (XPMP2 draws only the angle it is given); 0.4.6 steers nose wheels from the turn rate, speed and wheelbase; 0.4.7 eases the drawn height across lift-off and touchdown. 0.5.0 is the ClearanceLink name (ADR-0009).
- Traffic showcase (`docs/runbooks/traffic-showcase.md`): `clearancelink showcase` lines up one aircraft of each type on a runway, nose to tail and grouped Light to Heavy, one per remaining imported model (variants, freighters, other makers) on the parallel runway, and keeps 8 taxiing up and down a taxiway, through the plugin and without BeyondATC. Run at YBBN on 2026-10-04 (01R, 01L, taxiway S); it found most of the fixes below.
- FSLTL import (ADR-0005, `src/ClearanceLink.Fsltl`, research `docs/research/fsltl-models.md`): `clearancelink fsltl import` converts the user's own FSLTL install into an X-Plane CSL package in `Custom Data/CSL/ClearanceLink-FSLTL` (refuses while X-Plane runs); `serve` then sends BATC's exact livery key to the plugin. Ben's FSLTL 1.5.0: 3,112 liveries on 196 models, 13 GB at 2048 px. Checked in X-Plane at YBBN on 2026-10-03/04 with BATC traffic and the showcase: liveries, textures, faces, gear, wheels, steering and propellers right. Rules learned from those tests, all general: triangles face where their normals point; t = 1 - v; MSFS holds an animation's first and last keys and plays it over its own key range; animations no behaviour or declaration uses are ignored, and a fixed hold loses to a moving animation; unlisted gear and tyre animations are recognised by name; scaled parents are pushed into animated parts; textures without mipmaps get them; opaque materials get opaque alpha; nothing is blended (cut out instead) and propeller blur discs are left out, the still blades turning with the engine; steering is driven by XPMP2's nose wheel angle. Left out: Microsoft's Asobo models, the SU95 (730k triangles) and the SF34/SF34F (lopsided, see the check in FsltlImporter). Known: the FSPX MD-11's centre gear hangs above the ground in FSLTL's own model; the TFS Dash 8 ships 24 copies of one model.
